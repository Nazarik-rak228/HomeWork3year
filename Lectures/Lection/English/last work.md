  
1.  
Well, I spend more than twelve hours a day online. Most of that time goes on studying, doing homework at home, and just relaxing.  
I honestly think it’s normal nowadays. Almost everything in my life somehow involves the internet, so it doesn’t feel like too much.  
  
2.  
My favourite search engine is Google. I like it because the results are really well optimised and it has a cool AI mode.  
Moreover, I always use it in Firefox. That browser supports a lot of modifications and extensions much better than others, so the combination just works perfectly for me. Consequently, I don’t see any reason to switch.  
  
3.  
As a matter of fact, I search for answers to random or silly questions pretty often. This happens several times a day, whenever something curious or stupid pops into my head.  
In addition, it’s become a habit — my phone is always nearby, so I just quickly check whatever comes to mind.  
  
4.  
There’s so much information online that I always try to check a few different sources. I look at who wrote it and when it was published.  
Furthermore, if something seems important, I also compare it with official websites. That’s how I decide whether I can trust it or not.  
  
5.  
To be honest, I’ve never really worried about online security. I feel quite confident and experienced as a user.  
Of course the main risks are data theft, someone finding out my address, or losing money. However, I think those things mostly happen to people who are careless or too naive. With normal caution everything is usually fine.  
  
6.  
In my view, a child should be at least twelve or thirteen before getting free access to the internet. Younger kids can use it only under strict parental control and with clear time limits.  
Moreover, it’s also important to explain the dangers to them properly. Without that, early access can easily become a problem.  
  
7.  
I do know some older people who still never use the internet. Most of them have been living without it for many years.  
They prefer talking in real life and don’t feel that they need to use digital tools in their everyday lives. Sometimes it’s also because they don’t have the skills or simply don’t trust technology. For them, staying offline is just more comfortable.  
  
8.  
I’ve never really run a proper blog or built a serious website. I only played around a little with simple platforms out of curiosity.  
So I can’t say I have any real experience in that area yet. Nevertheless, I am interested in trying it properly in the future when I have more free time. In addition, learning the basics of website creation could be useful for my studies.  
  
9.  
Yes, I do feel connected through the internet — mostly to my friends and to some interesting communities. At the same time, I understand that this connection is often quite superficial.  
It can’t fully replace real conversations. Sometimes I still miss deeper offline communication. Therefore, I try to balance online and offline interaction.  
  
10.  
Most of the time, my internet is fast enough for studying, watching videos, and chatting. I don’t usually have any serious problems with its speed.  
However, I really notice when the internet starts working slowly. It immediately annoys me because it interrupts studying, watching something, or chatting. A slow connection just adds extra stress when I need to get things done. I start feeling irritated pretty quickly if it lasts more than a couple of minutes.  
  
11.  
The last time I had no internet was during a trip or when there were some technical problems. I don’t remember the exact date, but it wasn’t a very unusual situation.  
I usually switched to books, went outside, or just talked to people offline. Sometimes those moments even felt a bit refreshing after constant online noise. I actually managed to do things I normally postpone when the internet is available.  
  
12.  
I really like online shopping. It’s much more convenient for me than going to specialised stores.  
I mostly buy tools, materials for model-making, and sometimes the models themselves. It’s easier to find specific things online, especially when ordinary shops don’t have them.  
For ordinary things like food and clothes I still prefer regular shops. So I use both ways depending on what I need.  
  
13.  
I used to spend a lot of time on VKontakte, but lately I’ve been really drawn to X (Twitter). Its recommendation system quickly adjusted the whole feed to my interests and still keeps showing me new things.  
Furthermore, other networks have similar systems, but Twitter’s just works incredibly well for me. That’s why I prefer it now. As a result, I spend most of my social media time there.  
  
14.  
I love YouTube and often leave it playing in the background. I mainly watch game guides and recordings of streams that I can’t watch live because of the time difference. I also enjoy watching videos about games that I’m interested in.  
It’s both entertainment and a useful source of information for me. I can easily spend a couple of hours just listening to it while doing other things.  
  
15.  
My favourite app on the phone is definitely Telegram. I spend most of my screen time there just chatting with friends.  
Moreover, it’s basically my main way of staying in touch with people every day. Almost all my important conversations now happen there instead of regular calls or SMS. Therefore, I rarely use any other messenger.  
  
16.  
Yes, I play online games — it’s one of my main ways to relax after studying. I really love Tanks Blitz and War Thunder, and sometimes I also play CS2.  
Such games help me take a break from studies and simply enjoy the process. Furthermore, after a long study session they help me clear my head better than most other things. As a result, gaming has become an important part of my free time.  
  
17.  
I’ve never tried online dating. It feels strange to me to look for friends or a relationship with people I don’t know in real life. I prefer meeting people face to face.  
Of course the internet makes it easy to find like-minded people, but I always remember that you never really know who is behind the screen. I’ve heard enough stories about that.  
  
18.  
I haven’t personally met someone online and then met them in real life. However, I’ve heard of people who first met someone online and later met them in person.  
Quite often the person turned out different from what they expected. Stories like that only confirm for me that it’s better not to create too high expectations. I prefer to stay realistic about online acquaintances from the very beginning.  
  
19.  
I think medical services will never fully move online. The same goes for anything where you actually have to do something or receive something in real life.  
You just can’t replace a real doctor’s examination or physical procedures with a screen. Some things simply need a person to be present.  
  
20.  
To be honest, I’m not seriously interested in starting an online business right now. I’ve thought about it a little, mostly because the entry barrier looks quite low.  
But I haven’t chosen any specific area yet and don’t have concrete plans. I don’t have a particular business idea that I would like to develop at the moment.  
At the moment, this is a bad idea in our country. Just look at Lesta Games. Even if the idea sometimes appears, I don’t see real opportunities for myself at this point.
